# This channel's style: you are inside the story

The video plays the story out like an animated short, not a lecture. The viewer IS the hero: the narrator tells it in the second person ("You're twenty-three. You've just landed a job at Kodak."), characters talk to "you", and "you" answer back. The real person's name is revealed near the end ("In real life, that young engineer was named Steve Sasson.").

The recipe that works:

1. COLD OPEN AT THE END. Open on the hero years later, at the moment it all came out (the news on TV, a letter, a court room), in one or two beats. Then: "To understand how, we need to go back." For a what-if, open on the moment it goes wrong for you.
2. SCENES ARE EXCHANGES. Set the scene in one or two narrator beats, let the characters say two to four short lines to each other, then the narrator lands what it meant. About one beat in four is spoken by a character. Every main chapter has at least one exchange.
3. REACTIONS ARE LINES. "You" answers in one to four words where a real person would ("Using what?", "Twenty thousand?", "Thirteen?"). Deadpan beats comedy: an unimpressed reply after a big moment.
4. A RUNNING GAG. One object keeps coming back and means more each time (the drawer that swallows the prototype, then the warning report, then the patent). Use the `drawer` object or another story object for it.
5. A CALLBACK. A short line said early comes back at the end with a new meaning ("Needs work." about a photo, then about the whole company).
6. THE BIG NUMBERS ON CARDS. Real numbers still go on `stat` and `duo` cards with sources; the dialogue never carries a number that is not true.
7. THE ENDING. The narrator reveals the real name, says the lesson in one line, then turns it on the viewer with a question about their own life ("So, what's in your drawer?").

Dialogue and facts: characters are unnamed roles (boss, exec_a, woman, anchor), never a real person's name. A line may repeat what was really said or asked (a documented quote or question, in its spirit), or be ordinary talk that claims no fact ("Just see if it works."). Never invent a fact, number or decision through dialogue. The video's description will say the dialogue is dramatized.
